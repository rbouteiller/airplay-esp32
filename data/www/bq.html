Curves are computed with the same model the firmware uses, at the rate the I2S link is running. Faint lines are individual sections, bold is the cascade.

Every output that is driving a speaker, across both amplifiers, so a crossover split between them reads as one picture.

The sum is taken on the complex response, not on the curves, because two outputs feeding two drivers add as vectors. A crossover is right when the sum runs flat through the corner; a dip there means the branches are cancelling, so tick Inv on either one. This is the response of the filters alone; it knows nothing of the drivers, their spacing or the room.

Filter Chain

Load a frequency response export — REW text, or any file with a frequency and a level in its first two columns — and the fitter places filters to flatten it. Only the shape is fitted, never the level, and the two limits apply to the summed response rather than to any one filter.

The fit replaces every section past the keep count on the chain shown below, so measure with those first sections already playing: on a bi-amped speaker, keep the crossover and measure each way through it. The fit range matters more than anything else — a driver rolls off at its ends by more than any EQ can undo, and leaving the range wide spends filters fighting that instead of correcting the band the driver does cover.
